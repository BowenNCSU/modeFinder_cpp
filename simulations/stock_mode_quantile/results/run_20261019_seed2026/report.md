# Rolling mode / quantile trading simulation

Generated by `simulate.py` and `report.py`; the request is in `PROMPT.md`.

## Setup

- **Data:** 5-minute bars from `/Users/bowenliu/Downloads/data/5 min`.
- **Tickers:** 10 stocks and 5 ETFs, picked at random (seed 2026) among files with at least 5000 bars, a median close of at least $5 and a median dollar volume of at least $100,000 per bar.
- **Period:** trading starts once the longest window has filled (2026-05-13 21:55 to 2026-05-14 17:35, by ticker) and runs to the end of each file (2026-10-16 21:55 to 2026-10-16 21:55).
- **Capital:** $10,000 per ticker. The portfolio splits $10,000 equally across the tickers, and each part trades on its own.
- **Rolling fit:** at every bar, the window drops its oldest bar, adds the newest, and is refitted with `modefinder.ModeFinder`. Bernstein uses the support [min − 0.1·range, max + 0.1·range] of the window, and kernel uses R's `density()` defaults.
- **Grid:** price field open, close, high, low; window 78, 390, 1560 bars (78 bars ≈ one trading day); method Bernstein, kernel; rule mode, median, q25/q75, q10/q90, q05/q95; cost 0, 2 bps per buy or sell.
- **Rules:** long only, all in or all out. *mode* buys when the close is below the fitted mode and sells when it is above. *median* does the same with the fitted median. *qLL/qUU* buys below the LL% quantile and sells above the UU% quantile, holding in between.
- **Execution:** the decision uses the close of bar t and a fit on bars up to t, and fills at the open of bar t + 1. There is no look-ahead.

## Caveats

- **In-sample choice:** the best combination is picked on the same history it is scored on. Its edge over the others is an optimistic estimate; check it on another period or another sample of tickers.
- **Few tickers:** the sample is small and random, so a few tickers can drive the portfolio numbers.
- **Costs and fills:** fills at the next bar's open with a flat cost per trade. Spreads, slippage and market impact on illiquid names are not modelled beyond that.
- **Prices:** dividends are ignored and the data may have survivorship bias. Gaps between trading days count as one bar.
- **Cash:** idle cash earns nothing, and a long-only rule is out of the market part of the time, which lowers its return in a rising market.
